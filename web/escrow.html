Escrow

Buy or sell a domain without trusting the other side. The buyer's bitcoin is locked where nobody can take it alone: not the buyer, not the seller, not us. It is released when the domain arrives. If everyone walks away, a timer settles it after a set time, and the page shows you who that timer pays before you send a single sat.

    Who referees, if anyone

    An arbiter holds one key of three. They can co-sign with you or with your counterparty, never alone. We read both sides' published arbiter lists and offer only the ones you both accept.

  3. 3

    Where the domain is going

    The registry hides who owns a domain, so it can only show us which registrar holds it. As the buyer, say where you will receive it. "The transfer completed" will mean the registry shows exactly this.

    Your registrar's id is on IANA's list (GoDaddy 146, Namecheap 1068, Porkbun 1861, Cloudflare 1910). Prefer it: nameservers alone are weak, because the seller can point the domain at yours without handing it over. Use them only when you both use the same registrar, and whatever you commit, check that the domain is in your own registrar account before you release anything.

    You are the seller, so the buyer says where they will receive the domain. That comes back with their reply.

  4. 4

    Swap one message each way, then publish

    The escrow address needs both your keys, so neither of you can make it alone. Send your counterparty the invite link; they send back a short reply. Nothing in either message is secret, so you can paste them anywhere.

    This string is the only way to reclaim your money if we disappear. Write it down now. It holds your private key. We do not have a copy and cannot make you another one.
